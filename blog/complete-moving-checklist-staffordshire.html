The complete moving home checklist — Staffordshire 2026 edition

A complete moving home checklist isn't just a tick-list of tasks — it's a sequencing tool that puts each task in the week where it actually needs to happen. Done in the right order, a complete moving programme reduces a typical 3-bed Staffordshire move from an exhausting all-consuming month-long ordeal to a series of manageable weekend evenings plus one busy day. We've sequenced thousands of customer moves over 15 years and the checklist below distils the order and timing that consistently works. Print it, share it with your household, and tick items as you go.

Section 1

How to use this complete moving checklist

The checklist below is sequenced as a complete moving countdown from 8 weeks out to move day plus the first week after. Each phase covers a manageable batch of tasks — typically 1-2 weekends' worth of work — and the sequencing matters because some tasks can't happen until earlier ones are complete. The decluttering has to happen before the packing; the packing has to happen before the lorry arrives; the address-change notifications need to be sent before the post-redirect kicks in.

Print this complete moving checklist and share it with everyone in your household — partner, teenage kids who can help, parents who might be visiting to help on move day. Moving is a household project, not an individual one; the customers who complete the checklist as a team have noticeably lower stress at every phase than the ones where one person carries the whole moving load.

Adjust the timeline if your move date is closer: 8 weeks is the ideal lead time. If you have 4 weeks, compress phases 1-3 into the first 2 weeks; if you have 2 weeks, accept that some tasks won't get done and prioritise the financially/legally critical ones (remover booking, utilities transfer, council tax notification). Our wider move planning guide covers the 12-week version if you have more lead time.

Section 2

8 weeks before move day

☐ Decide on professional vs DIY removal. Most family moves are professional; small 1-bed moves often DIY. See our DIY-vs-professional comparison.

☐ Request 3-4 removal quotes from established Staffordshire providers. Surveyed quotes only — phone quotes for any home over 1-bed are almost always wrong.

☐ Decide packing service tier: full, fragile-only, DIY, or materials-only. See our packing service guide.

☐ Start the declutter — one room per weekend, working from easiest (loft, garage, spare room) to hardest (kitchen, bedrooms with sentimental items). See our declutter guide.

☐ Open the running 'address-change list' — paper or app. Every account/service/person needing notification goes on the list as you remember them. Expect 25-40 items by week 4.

☐ Decide on storage requirement. If yes, request storage quote alongside removal quote.

Section 3

6 weeks before move day

☐ Choose and book your remover. Sign the quote, pay the deposit (typically 10-20%), confirm the booking date in writing.

☐ Continue declutter — donations and bulky-waste removal happen this week and next.

☐ Order packing materials if DIY-packing. Our packaging shop has property-size kits.

☐ Start collecting paperwork for completion — solicitor contact details, mortgage offer details (if buying), insurance documents.

☐ If you're moving with pets: book the cattery, kennels or pet-sitter for move day. See our pets checklist.

☐ If you're moving with school-age children: confirm the new school enrolment, get the start date confirmed in writing.

Section 4

4 weeks before move day

☐ Notify utilities at the current property (gas, electric, water): book final meter reading dates around move day. Cancel broadband / arrange transfer.

☐ Set up utilities at the new property: choose providers, confirm start dates. Get broadband installation booked for the day after move day — installer waits are 7-21 days typically, so this can't be left to move week.

☐ Council tax notification: notify the current council you're leaving (gives them final meter and balance), notify the new council you're arriving (gives them your start date for billing).

☐ Bank, credit cards, mortgage provider, insurance policies: address change. Most online, takes 5-10 minutes each.

☐ DVLA: change of address for driving licence (free, online) and vehicle V5 logbook (free, online — keep the V5 in hand for any vehicle transactions).

☐ HMRC, employer payroll, pension provider: address change.

☐ GP, dentist, optician: address change (may also need to register at new GP if moving more than a few miles).

☐ Subscriptions: Amazon, eBay, magazine subs, charity direct debits, streaming services, gym membership.

Section 5

2 weeks before move day

☐ Start DIY packing if applicable: begin with non-essentials (loft contents, garage, spare rooms, off-season clothing, books and DVDs).

☐ Labelling system: every carton gets room of destination + contents summary + priority code (Day 1 / Week 1 / Long-term). Label both top and one side so labels stay visible when stacked.

☐ Book the Royal Mail Redirect service (online, £33.99 for 3 months, £52.99 for 12 months) — schedule to start on move day. Catches everything you forgot to notify.

☐ Defrost-prep plan: run down freezer contents this week so the defrost in week 1 is easier.

☐ Charity shop final run: last opportunity to drop off donations before move week.

☐ Final confirmation with remover: date, time, crew size, contacts on the day, vehicle parking arrangement at both properties.

☐ Confirm completion date with solicitor and estate agent — chase if not yet firmed up.

Section 6

1 week before move day

☐ Continue packing — kitchen contents (except move-day essentials), final clothing, bathroom contents, anything been in daily use you can live without for 7-10 days.

☐ First-night box: pack a single box of move-day essentials that travels in YOUR CAR, not the lorry. Kettle, mugs, tea/coffee, milk, sugar, biscuits, loo roll, soap, towels, paracetamol, kids' essentials, phone chargers, takeaway menus for the new area, the new property keys when received, kids' favourite toys/comfort items.

☐ Move-day household plan: where everyone (humans, pets, kids' toys) will be at each stage of the day. Written down somewhere everyone can see.

☐ Final clean at the current property: at least the bathrooms, kitchen and obvious problem areas. Move-out condition affects deposit return for renters and sale-completion documentation for owners.

☐ Defrost the freezer if you're taking it — 24-48 hours before move day, with the door propped open to fully dry.

☐ Confirm pet/childcare arrangements for move day.

☐ Run final laundry loads so all clothes are clean and packed for arrival.

Section 7

Move day itself

Morning (7-9am): ☐ Final house walk-through. ☐ Confirm meter readings (photograph the meter dials). ☐ First-night box loaded into your car. ☐ Kids and pets relocated to their move-day arrangement.

Crew arrival (typically 8-9am): ☐ Walk-through with crew lead. ☐ Confirm loading order, floor protection, any specific access notes. ☐ Confirm new-property address and meeting plan.

Loading (typically 9am-1pm): ☐ Stay reachable but not in the way. ☐ Lock final doors and windows as rooms are emptied. ☐ Final fridge/freezer check (everything removed). ☐ Return any keys to landlord/agent if renting; collect new-property keys.

Travel to new property: ☐ Follow the lorry if local; otherwise meet at the new property. Don't ride in the cab — only 2 seats and you need flexibility.

Unloading at new property (typically 2-6pm): ☐ Walk through with crew lead, confirm room destinations. ☐ Direct items to right rooms as they unload. ☐ Beds assembled first (so you have somewhere to fall over at end of day). ☐ Sign-off on inventory before crew leaves.

End of move day: ☐ Settle final balance with remover. ☐ Take meter readings at new property (photograph). ☐ Get takeaway. ☐ Find the bathroom essentials, towels and bedding. ☐ Sleep.

Section 8

First week after move day

Day 1 (morning after): ☐ Confirm utilities are working (electric, gas, water, broadband if scheduled). ☐ Locate kitchen essentials. ☐ Confirm new GP / school / dentist registrations.

Day 2-3: ☐ Unpack kitchen and bathrooms completely. ☐ Unpack bedrooms (clothes go away properly). ☐ Confirm post is arriving (or that the redirect is working).

Day 4-7: ☐ Unpack remaining rooms gradually — living room, study, kids' bedrooms. ☐ Break down empty cartons (some companies, including us, collect empties for recycling at no charge in the first week). ☐ Confirm all utilities switched correctly (gas + electric supplier transfer, council tax start date).

By end of week 1: ☐ Walk the property and note any post-move issues (damage, missing items, anything that needs attention from the remover). ☐ Contact the remover about any concerns — most damage claims are easier to handle in the first week than later. ☐ Photo any issues for reference.

Week 2 onwards: ☐ Unpack remaining cartons at a leisurely pace. ☐ Tackle decoration and minor improvements. ☐ Update remaining address-change items as they surface (mail forwarded by Royal Mail catches them as they arrive). ☐ Add any not-yet-set-up subscriptions or services.

Section 9

Books, paperwork and what to keep handy

Three categories of paperwork need to be accessible THROUGHOUT the move and the first week, not packed deep in a labelled carton.

Move-day essential paperwork: remover's contact details (phone + address), completion paperwork from solicitor, current and new property keys, ID for proof-of-address purposes (driving licence with current OR new address — DVLA usually allows a 2-3 week grace period). All in a single 'move folder' that travels with you, not in the lorry.

First-week reference paperwork: utility transfer reference numbers (gas, electric, water, broadband), council tax notification confirmations, any insurance policy renewal dates approaching, bank/credit-card change-of-address confirmations. Keep accessible for the first month.

Long-term storage paperwork: the remover's inventory (signed at move day), invoice and payment confirmation, any damage notes from move-day walkthroughs. Keep filed safely with home contents insurance documents — relevant if any claim surfaces in the months after.

FAQ

Common questions

How early should I start using a moving checklist?

8 weeks before move day is ideal; 4 weeks is workable with compression; 2 weeks means accepting that some tasks won't get done (prioritise remover booking, utilities, council tax, mortgage/insurance address changes). The earlier you start, the lower the move-week stress.

What's the single most-forgotten item on most moving checklists?

Utility transfers booked too late. Gas and electric transfers between providers typically take 17-21 days to process; broadband transfers take 7-14 days; doing these in move week often means days without power or internet at the new property. Schedule utility changes at the 4-week mark, not the final week.

Should I pack the kitchen first or last?

Last. Kitchen is the most-used room until the final day, and the contents are the most fragile to pack. Pack non-essentials first (loft, garage, off-season clothes), then bedroom contents, then living room, then bathrooms, then kitchen in the final 1-2 weeks.

What's a first-night box and why does it matter?

A single box of move-day essentials (kettle, mugs, tea/coffee, milk, biscuits, loo roll, soap, towels, paracetamol, phone chargers, kids' essentials, takeaway menus) that travels in YOUR CAR not the lorry. Means you have basics even if the lorry is delayed or unloading runs long. Single most-recommended item in our post-move customer feedback.

How long does post-move unpacking actually take?

Realistic timeline: kitchen and bathrooms unpacked in 2-3 days, bedrooms in 3-5 days, remaining rooms over 1-2 weeks. Full unpacking and settling-in is typically a 2-4 week process, not move-day-and-done. Don't plan major activities for the first week after move day.
