Plan your move — a 12-week move planning guide for Staffordshire

Move planning is one of those things where the cost of getting it wrong is paid twice — once in the chaos of the move week itself, and again in the weeks afterwards as you discover what wasn't packed, what wasn't connected, and what wasn't booked. We've watched somewhere north of 4,000 customer moves play out across Staffordshire since 2010, and the difference between the smooth ones and the painful ones is almost always upstream of move day — it's the move planning, the lead time, and the realistic expectations the customer set themselves about how much work moving genuinely involves. This guide walks through the practical 12-week move planning countdown we recommend to every Staffordshire customer who has the lead time, what to do in each phase, the planning mistakes we see most often, and how to recover when the move plan starts to slip.

Section 1

Why move planning matters more than people think

Most people move house roughly every 7-10 years in the UK; the experience doesn't accumulate enough for any single move to feel routine. Each move is the first move in a long time, which means each move recreates the same planning mistakes from scratch — under-estimating the volume of contents, leaving the remover booking too late, forgetting the address-change admin until move week, and discovering that one obvious thing nobody planned for. Move planning is the discipline that prevents these mistakes; the customers who plan their move thoroughly are consistently the customers who report the lowest stress in our post-move feedback.

The other half of the move planning argument is financial. A planned move costs less than an unplanned one — by a meaningful margin. Last-minute removal bookings command higher rates (peak slots that planners booked weeks ago are gone), urgent packing materials cost more than supplied-in-advance bulk orders, missed council tax or utility notifications cause overlapping bills, and emergency cleaning or repair work commands premium rates. A well-planned 3-bed Staffordshire move typically costs £200-£500 less than the same move booked last-minute, before counting the time-cost of the stress.

The good news: move planning is genuinely teachable, and the framework we recommend has stayed essentially the same for 15 years. The 12-week countdown below covers what to do, when, and why, with the milestones that catch most customers out flagged in advance. Our 2026 cost guide covers the budget side; this piece is about the planning side.

Section 2

Weeks 12-10: foundations

If you know you're moving in roughly three months' time, the first move planning task is to set the rough envelope. Pick a target move month (not yet a specific date — the date will firm up later), and start the high-level decisions that need lead time: are you using a professional remover or doing it DIY, are you packing yourself or paying for packing, what's the budget envelope, will you need storage between properties.

Decisions to make in this window: remover vs DIY (most family moves are professional; small flat moves often DIY); packing service tier (full / fragile-only / DIY); storage requirement (yes/no and rough volume); insurance position (declared-value uplift needed?); whether you'll be present for the entire move or relying on a project manager.

What to start in this window: request 3-4 removal quotes from established Staffordshire providers, start the declutter (the single most-skipped phase of move planning — see our declutter guide), and start the 'paperwork list' — the running list of accounts, services, and contacts that'll need an address-change notification later.

Section 3

Weeks 10-8: booking the remover

By the 8-week mark, you should have your removal company chosen and booked. For move dates in the May-September peak, booking later than this often means the preferred Friday slot has already gone. For off-peak months (October-April), 4-6 weeks ahead is usually enough.

Survey: the remover should survey your property before quoting — most do this in person (60-minute home visit); some accept video walk-throughs for smaller moves. Insist on a survey rather than a phone-quote — phone quotes for anything beyond a 1-bed flat are almost always wrong by enough to matter.

Written quote: the quote should be fixed-price (not hourly), valid for 60 days, itemised so you can see what's included, and include insurance details (Goods in Transit and Public Liability limits in writing). Our 'choosing a reliable remover' guide covers the verification questions to ask before booking.

Deposit: most established Staffordshire removers take a deposit at booking (typically 10-20% of the total) with the balance on completion. Reputable companies don't ask for full payment upfront — that's a warning sign worth walking away from.

Section 4

Weeks 8-6: declutter and downsize

The 8-6 week window is when serious decluttering happens. The principle of move planning is simple: the less you move, the less you pay, and the less you have to unpack at the other end. Most Staffordshire households shed 15-30% of their possessions during a thorough pre-move declutter, and the financial saving on the move itself often covers a meaningful chunk of the moving cost.

Room-by-room approach: tackle one room per weekend across this 2-week window. Start with the easiest (loft, garage, spare room) and finish with the hardest (kitchen, bedrooms with sentimental items). Sort everything into keep / donate / sell / dispose piles as you go.

Disposal logistics: charity shop pick-ups for furniture; council bulky-waste collection for the larger non-recyclable items; recycling centre runs for everything that doesn't fit either; eBay or Facebook Marketplace for the genuinely valuable. Allow 2-3 weekends — disposal is rarely a one-trip job.

Sentimental items: the slowest category. Plan to discuss with family members during this window which items have meaning to whom, so the eventual moving decision is informed rather than rushed.

Section 5

Weeks 6-4: paperwork and admin

The 6-4 week window is the paperwork phase of move planning. The list of accounts, services, and contacts that need address-change notifications is longer than most people expect — typically 25-40 different organisations across a typical move.

Essentials list: bank and savings accounts, credit cards, mortgage provider (if buying), insurance policies (home, car, life, pet), employer payroll, HMRC, DVLA (driving licence and vehicle V5), TV licence, GP surgery, dentist, optician, school enrolment (if children changing schools), pension provider, council tax (both councils), Land Registry property registration, utilities at both addresses (gas, electric, water, broadband, mobile), Royal Mail redirect service.

Subscription services: Amazon, eBay, online retailers, magazine subscriptions, charity direct debits, Netflix/streaming, gym membership, professional association fees.

Practical contacts: family and friends (don't rely on them just-figuring-it-out), tradespeople you use regularly (window cleaner, gardener, cleaner), kids' clubs and activities, local business loyalty programmes.

The Royal Mail Redirect service catches everything you miss for £33.99 (3 months) or £52.99 (12 months) — well worth the cost as the backstop for the dozen organisations you'll forget. Worth setting up to start on move day. Our hidden costs guide covers the full list of address-change costs.

Section 6

Weeks 4-2: packing prep

The 4-2 week window is when packing starts in earnest if you're DIY-packing. Start with non-essentials (loft contents, garage, spare rooms, off-season clothing, books you don't need for the next 4 weeks) and work toward essentials. Most family households need 50-100 cartons for a 3-bed move; ordering packing materials in this window gives you time to do the work without panic.

Carton sourcing: our packaging shop sells full kits sized to property type (1-bed kit £40-£70; 3-bed kit £120-£180). Free recycled cartons from supermarkets and high-street retailers work for non-fragile items but aren't strong enough for books or kitchen contents.

Labelling system: every carton gets a room destination AND a brief contents summary AND a priority indicator (Day 1 essentials, Week 1 useful, Long-term storage). The labelling system feels excessive in week 4 and pays off when you arrive at the new property in week 0.

Packing service decision: if you've booked professional packing, the packing visit happens in the final week. If you're DIY-ing, this window is when 80% of the packing should happen. Our packing service guide covers the full-vs-fragile-vs-DIY decision.

Storage logistics: if you're using storage between properties, confirm the storage booking and access arrangements in this window. Our storage comparison guide covers the options.

Section 7

Weeks 2-1: confirmation and preparation

The penultimate week is the confirmation phase. Confirm with the remover (date, time, crew size, contacts on the day, any specific access notes about either property). Confirm completion date with solicitors and estate agents — chase if it's not yet firmed up. Confirm utility transfers (final meter readings booked, new-property suppliers chosen and start-date set).

First-night box: pack a single box of move-day essentials that travels in your car, not in the lorry. Suggested contents: kettle, mugs, tea/coffee, milk, sugar, biscuits, loo roll, soap, towels, paracetamol, kids' essentials (if applicable), phone chargers, takeaway menus for the area you're moving to, the new property keys (when you get them), and the kids' favourite toys/comfort items.

Final week packing: kitchen contents (apart from move-day essentials in your first-night box), final clothing, bathroom contents, anything that's been in daily use that you can now live without for 7-10 days.

Move-day plan: walked through with everyone in the household. Where the kids will be (school, with grandparents, with you), where the pets will be (boarded, with friends, in a single closed room with food and water), where you'll meet the crew, who has the keys, who's supervising at the new property.

Section 8

The move week itself

The day before: defrost the freezer (if you're taking it), final clean of the worst rooms, run final laundry loads, pack the first-night box. Confirm crew arrival time once more. Get an early night.

Move day morning: the crew typically arrives 8-9am. They'll walk through the property with you, confirm the loading order, lay floor protection, and start. Most 3-bed Staffordshire moves load by 11am-12pm and depart between then and 1pm.

Travel between properties: if the new property is local, follow the lorry. If it's longer distance, agree a meeting point and time. Don't ride in the lorry — there's typically only 2 cab seats and you need to be flexible.

Move day at the new property: the crew unloads, you direct items to the right rooms (your room-labelling system from week 4 pays off here), and the basics get set up (beds, kitchen essentials, bathroom). Most moves complete unload by 4-6pm.

End of day: sign-off on the inventory, settle the balance if not already paid, get a takeaway, sleep. Unpacking is a problem for tomorrow.

Section 9

Common move planning mistakes

Three planning mistakes recur on enough moves that they're worth flagging in advance.

1. Under-estimating volume. Most customers' mental model of their contents is significantly smaller than the actual contents. A surveyor walking the property in week 8 usually estimates 15-30% more volume than the customer would have. Trust the surveyor's figure; book the vehicle and crew for the surveyor's volume, not yours.

2. Leaving the declutter too late. Decluttering in week 1 doesn't work — there's no time to dispose of items, charity shops don't accept last-minute drops, and the customer ends up paying to move belongings they could have shed. Start the declutter in week 8 at latest.

3. Forgetting the address-change list. The list above looks long but each individual notification takes 5-10 minutes. Done over 4 weeks (one batch per week), it's manageable. Done in move week, it's overwhelming and things get missed — usually surfacing as a chase-letter from an unexpected creditor 3-6 months later.

Bonus mistake: booking utilities to switch on move-out day rather than the day before/after. Schedule electricity and water for HALF A DAY OVERLAP at both ends so the crew arrives to a powered, water-on property regardless of timing surprises.

Section 10

When the plan changes — late completions and last-minute swaps

Despite the best move planning, completions slip and dates change. Solicitors don't release keys, money transfers don't clear, the chain hits a last-minute issue. Approximately 5% of our moves experience some completion-day delay; about 1% extend overnight. Knowing the recovery options reduces the stress.

Late key release on move day: our standard policy is up to 3 hours of waiting at no charge. Longer waits are discussed but we never invoice for completion delays. If keys aren't released by 4pm, your loaded contents go into our depot overnight free of charge, with redelivery first thing next morning.

Completion date change in the lead-up: we re-schedule for free with 7+ days notice; smaller fee for 3-7 day notice; emergency arrangements within 72 hours are handled case-by-case with the smallest possible cost impact. Customer-side date changes don't trigger penalty charges — life happens, chains slip, we'd rather find a workable solution than recover lost cost.

Contents change at the last minute (e.g. discovery of more loft contents than estimated, decision to take the garden shed as well): we'll usually accommodate small increases for free; larger increases may need a bigger vehicle and a small uplift but never a different price band. Communicate the change as soon as it's known.

The wider principle: good move planning isn't about planning the perfect move — it's about building enough margin into the plan that imperfections don't derail it. Our seasonal timing guide covers the related question of which months have the most planning margin.

FAQ

Common questions

How far in advance should I start planning my move?

12 weeks ideal, 8 weeks workable, 4 weeks compressed-but-doable. Peak May-September moves need earliest planning because remover availability tightens 4-6 weeks ahead. Off-peak October-April moves can compress to 4 weeks lead time without losing too much margin.

What's the single most-skipped move planning task?

The pre-move declutter. Most customers leave it until week 1 or 2 of the move, by which point there's no time to dispose of items properly, charity shops can't accept last-minute drops, and you end up paying to move belongings you could have shed. Start the declutter at week 8 latest.

How do I plan around a slipping completion date?

Build flexibility into the plan from the start: book a remover with a clear date-change policy (we re-schedule free with 7+ days notice); arrange utilities for half-day overlap at both ends rather than exact move-day timing; keep the first-night box and move-day essentials accessible separately from the rest of the contents; plan to be at the new property for at least 24 hours after move day to handle any post-move issues.

What paperwork should I have ready for move day?

Move folder containing: remover's contact details, completion paperwork from solicitor, current and new property keys, ID for proof-of-address (driving licence). Folder travels with you, not in the lorry. Keep utility-transfer reference numbers and council-tax confirmations accessible for the first month.

Should I book a removal company before or after my completion date is firm?

Before — book the remover 6-8 weeks ahead with a target date that may shift by a week either way. Most established removers (including us) hold the booking with date-flexibility and reschedule for free if your completion date slips within 7+ days notice. Booking only after completion is firm often means the preferred slots have gone.
