Section 1

How to read this top tips guide

These eight tips for moving house aren't sequenced by week — they're sequenced by importance, with the highest-impact recommendations first. Implement the top three tips and you'll already have eliminated 60-70% of the avoidable stress of a typical move. Implement all eight and you'll be moving house at the level of a customer with three previous moves under their belt.

Each tip explains both WHAT to do and WHY it works, because moving house is a project where understanding the reason behind the advice helps you adapt when your situation doesn't exactly match the standard template. A 4-bed family move with three children is different from a 1-bed retired couple's downsize, but the underlying principles in these tips apply to both.

The most experienced movers we know — our own crew members who've moved house themselves 5-15 times over their careers — converge on roughly the same shortlist of priorities below. Trust the consistency: these tips work because they reflect repeated lived experience, not theoretical advice.

Section 2

Tip 1: start planning at least 8 weeks before move day

The most important top tip for moving house is also the most boring: start planning early. The majority of move-day stress comes from compressed timelines — tasks that should be done in week 6 left until week 1, decisions that should be made in week 4 made on move-day morning, and admin that should be spread across 4 weeks crammed into the final week.

What 'start planning' means in practice: by 8 weeks out you should have requested 3-4 removal quotes, decided on professional vs DIY, started the running address-change list, and started the room-by-room declutter. None of these are big-effort tasks individually; cumulatively over 8 weeks they make the difference between a smooth move and a chaotic one.

What if you don't have 8 weeks? Most of the framework still applies — just compressed. With 4 weeks lead time, do weeks 8-6 in your first weekend, weeks 6-4 in your second weekend, weeks 4-2 in your third weekend, and the final 2 weeks as planned. With 2 weeks, accept that some optimisation passes won't happen and prioritise the financially/legally critical tasks (remover booking, utilities, council tax, mortgage/insurance address changes). Our 12-week move planning guide covers the full version.

Section 3

Tip 2: get multiple quotes and verify what each one includes

Removal quotes vary by 30-50% between providers for the same job, and the variance is almost never about quality vs price tradeoffs — it's about what's included. The cheap quote that excludes packing materials, insurance to professional levels, wardrobe boxes, and dismantling/reassembly works out more expensive than the mid-priced quote that includes everything once the extras are added on the day.

What to verify before accepting a quote: is it fixed-price (not hourly)? Is it valid for 60 days? Does it include all packing materials, fuel, insurance to specified limits, parking permits, wardrobe boxes on the day, dismantling and reassembly of standard furniture? Are there any extras commonly added on the day? Our 'choosing reliable remover' guide covers the verification questions in detail.

The cheapest quote is almost never the right answer. Honest-priced quotes for a typical 3-bed Staffordshire move cluster around £650-£900 in 2026; quotes materially below £550 usually have hidden exclusions; quotes above £1,000 are usually from corporate or brokerage providers with higher overheads. The middle of the honest market is the right price for the right service. See our 2026 cost guide.

Section 4

Tip 3: declutter ruthlessly before you pack a single box

The single biggest opportunity to reduce moving cost and post-move unpacking workload is the pre-move declutter. The principle is simple: every item you don't move is an item you don't pay to move and don't have to unpack at the other end. Most Staffordshire households shed 15-30% of their possessions during a thorough declutter, and the financial saving on the move itself often covers the equivalent of a week's worth of new-home decorating.

Top-tip decluttering rule: if you haven't used it in the last 2 years and it doesn't have specific sentimental value, it doesn't make the trip. Sentimental value is a legitimate exception; 'might-use-someday' is not.

Three-category sort: Keep / Donate-or-Sell / Dispose. Don't add intermediate categories — they become procrastination buckets. Things that don't qualify for Keep go into Donate-or-Sell (everything functional) or Dispose (broken or low-value). Decisions get easier with practice and faster as you work room by room.

Tackle the easiest rooms first (loft, garage, spare room) to build momentum, then progressively harder rooms (kitchen, bedrooms). Sentimental rooms (with photo albums, family heirlooms, kids' baby stuff) tackle last when you've practiced the sorting muscle. Our dedicated declutter guide covers the room-by-room approach in detail.

Section 5

Tip 4: label every box with destination room AND contents AND priority

The labelling system that experienced movers use has three parts: room of destination (which room at the new property), contents summary (one or two phrases — 'kitchen utensils', 'kids bedroom toys'), and priority code (Day 1, Week 1, Long-term). Every box gets all three on both the top AND one side panel.

Why all three matter: destination room directs the unload (crew puts boxes in the right rooms automatically); contents summary lets you find the kettle without opening 15 boxes; priority code tells you which boxes to unpack first (Day 1 essentials) vs last (long-term stuff you can unpack at leisure).

Why labels go on both top and side: when boxes are stacked, the top label may not be visible. The side label is what the crew reads when looking at a stack.

Numbering and inventory: for moves with significant value, number each box and keep a master inventory (box 1 = kitchen Day 1 = kettle + mugs + cutlery). The inventory becomes the master reference if anything's lost or damaged. Most household moves don't need this level of detail; antiques and high-value moves benefit substantially. Our packing service guide covers the wider labelling and inventory considerations.

Section 6

Tip 5: pack a first-night box that travels in YOUR car, not the lorry

The first-night box is the single most-recommended top tip in our customer post-move feedback. The principle: a single box of move-day essentials travels in YOUR CAR (not in the lorry), so that even if the lorry hasn't arrived or unloading is delayed, your household has the basics for the first evening.

First-night box contents: kettle, mugs, tea/coffee, milk, sugar, biscuits, loo roll, soap, towels, paracetamol, kids' essentials (bottles, nappies, comfort toys), phone chargers, the new property keys (when you get them), takeaway menus for the area you're moving to.

What also travels with you: a small toiletries bag for each household member with toothbrush, deodorant, basic toiletries; a single change of clothes each; the move folder (paperwork, IDs, important documents); any valuables you've chosen not to send with the lorry (jewellery, important papers, cash, prescription medications).

What this prevents: the classic move-day failure mode where you arrive at the new property exhausted, the lorry is delayed, and you can't make a cup of tea or wash your face because everything's packed. The first-night box converts this from a crisis into a routine setup.

Section 7

Tip 6: photograph electronics setups before disconnecting them

Before you disconnect any electronics — TV, sound system, home computer, kitchen appliances with multiple cable connections, washing machine plumbing — take a photograph of how they're connected. The photograph becomes the reference when you're setting them up again at the new property, and saves a remarkable amount of frustration trying to remember which cable went into which port.

Particularly important for: TVs with HDMI/optical/coax connections (modern TVs can have 6+ cables), sound systems with speaker wiring, home computers with monitor/printer/external-drive cables, kitchen appliances with both power and water connections (washing machine, dishwasher).

Photographs to take: wide shot of the back of the device showing all connections, close-ups of any unusual or specific connection patterns, photographs of cable management (e.g. routing through furniture). Store the photographs in a 'move setup' album on your phone for easy reference.

Beyond electronics: photographs are also useful for furniture assemblies you might want to reproduce exactly at the new property (specific kitchen pantry organisation, bookshelf layout, garage tool storage). Most reassembly is easier with a reference photograph than without.

Section 8

Tip 7: notify utilities and councils at least 4 weeks before move day

Utilities and council tax notifications are time-sensitive in a way most moving-house top tips don't make clear. Gas and electric transfers between providers typically take 17-21 days to process; broadband transfers can take 7-14 days; council tax notifications need to align with move date to avoid overlapping bills. Done with 4+ weeks notice, these are routine; done in the final week, things go wrong.

Order to notify: council tax (both old council to confirm move-out date, and new council to register move-in date) — these are usually online forms taking 5-10 minutes each; gas and electric (final meter reading booked for move day at old property, account opened at new property with start date); water (final meter reading at old, new account at new); broadband (cancel at old, arrange installation at new — broadband installer waits are 7-21 days so book early); TV licence (free address change online).

Royal Mail Redirect service catches everything you forget: £33.99 for 3 months or £52.99 for 12 months, schedules to start on move day, forwards your mail from old to new address. Worth setting up in week 2 to be active by move day. Our hidden costs guide covers the full address-change list.

The 25-40 address-change items list: bank, savings, credit cards, mortgage, all insurance (home, car, life, pet), employer payroll, HMRC, DVLA (licence + V5), GP, dentist, optician, schools, subscriptions (Amazon, eBay, magazines, streaming, charity direct debits, gym), family and friends. Spread across 4 weeks, one batch per week, this is manageable.

Section 9

Tip 8: be flexible on moving day — things will take longer than expected

The final top tip for moving house is psychological: be flexible on moving day. Moves run longer than scheduled more often than they don't, completion dates slip more often than they hold, and the inevitable small problems that come up are solvable but they need responding to with calm rather than panic.

What 'flexibility' means in practice: don't book important meetings for move-day evening; don't promise to be at a specific time-bound event the morning after; don't schedule the kids' birthday party for move day plus one; don't expect to be unpacked by end of move day; don't expect the new property's broadband to work on move day (installers often book a day later).

Build buffer into the plan: book a takeaway for move-day dinner (no cooking needed); have at least one easy meal for the first morning at the new property (cereal, toast); accept that the first week is for getting basics in place, not for redecoration or improvements.

The customer attitude that gets the best outcome from a removal crew: clear about what you want, reasonable about timing slippage, communicative when something changes, and grateful for the work being done. Removal crews are doing 8-12 hours of heavy physical work on your behalf; the customer attitude makes a meaningful difference to the quality of execution.
