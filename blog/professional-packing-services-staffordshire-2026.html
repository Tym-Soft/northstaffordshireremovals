Professional packing services in Staffordshire — full, partial or DIY in 2026

Most customers booking a house move in Staffordshire reach the same decision point: am I packing this myself, paying you to do the fragile-only stuff, or paying you to do the lot? It's not a small decision — full packing typically adds 25–40% to the move price, but it also reclaims the better part of a working week of evenings before the move, removes the single biggest stress driver of moving day, and substantially improves the insurance position on anything that breaks in transit. Section 1

What "professional packing" actually means

Professional packing is the part of a removal service where someone other than you wraps, boxes, labels and inventories the contents of your home before move day. It's distinct from the wrapping of large furniture (which all removal crews do on the day as part of the lift), and it's distinct from the unpacking at the destination (which is a separate add-on most customers don't take). What you're paying for is the boxes-and-tissue work — the dozens of hours of kitchen, bedroom, bathroom, study and loft contents being individually wrapped and packed into properly-labelled cartons.

The reason it costs material money is not because the materials are expensive — boxes and tissue paper are cheap. It's because the labour is significant: a typical 3-bed home takes a two-person crew 6–10 hours to pack to a professional standard, depending on how much fragile content there is, how organised the household already is, and whether the crew is wrapping each item individually (full pack) or just kitchen-and-glass (fragile-only). At our standard packing rates that's £160–£320 of pure labour cost before materials.

What's actually included in a professional pack: kitchen contents wrapped individually in archival paper, fragile-only items double-wrapped in tissue then bubble wrap, breakable items packed into specialised dish-pack cartons (smaller than standard boxes with internal cell dividers), book boxes for books (smaller than standard so they're not too heavy to lift safely), wardrobe boxes for hanging clothes (collected at the same visit), mattress covers, picture-frame packs for art and mirrors, full carton labelling by destination room with contents summary, and a written carton inventory the customer signs off.

What's not included as standard: sorting through your own possessions to decide what's worth packing (that's your declutter, not our job), packing of perishable food (we'll move sealed-pantry items but not fridge/freezer contents), packing of items with hazardous content (paint, solvents, propane cylinders), and packing of items you've specifically marked 'pack yourself' (your private papers, jewellery, the contents of a single closet you've earmarked as yours).

Section 2

The three service tiers — full vs partial vs DIY

Three distinct service tiers cover the spectrum of how much packing you delegate. Each has its own price point, its own time commitment from you, and its own insurance implications.

Tier 1: Full packing service. Our crew packs everything in the property — kitchen, bedrooms, study, garage, loft, the lot. Typical timing: a two-person crew arrives 1–2 days before move day, spends 6–10 hours packing a 3-bed home, returns on move day to load the now-packed cartons alongside the unpacked furniture. By move day morning, everything is in labelled boxes and the only thing left for you to do is direct the crew at the destination. Price typically £350–£700 added to a standard 3-bed move quote.

Tier 2: Fragile-only packing service. Our crew packs the genuinely-breakable items — kitchen china and glassware, framed art and mirrors, ornaments and porcelain, specific electronics customers point out. Everything else (clothes, books, bedding, soft furnishings, non-breakable kitchen contents) the customer packs themselves. This is the most popular tier we sell because it lifts the highest-skill packing off the customer while leaving the routine packing as a low-risk DIY job. Price typically £180–£380 added to a standard 3-bed move quote.

Tier 3: DIY packing. Customer packs everything themselves; our crew only handles the move day itself (load, transport, unload). The cheapest option and what most people do for smaller moves and budget-conscious moves. We can supply the materials separately (£40–£120 for a typical 3-bed kit of boxes, tape and paper) so you're using the right cartons even if you're packing them yourself. No labour cost added to the move quote.

A practical middle ground: some customers book full packing only for specific rooms — typically the kitchen and the master bedroom — and do the rest themselves. We'll quote that as a sub-tier (typically £120–£240 added) at survey if that's the right shape for your move. Service page has more detail on the combinations.

Section 3

When each tier wins (and when it loses)

Full pack wins when: you have limited time before move day (a tight completion date, a demanding job, young children or caring responsibilities), the household contains significant fragile or valuable content (china, art, glass, ceramics, instruments), you're moving from a larger property (4+ bedrooms) where DIY packing would take 30+ hours of your time, you're moving internationally (where insurance positions on self-packed items are restricted), or you simply have the budget and would rather not spend evenings wrapping plates.

Full pack loses when: the move is small (1-bed flat, studio, partial-house move), the household contents are minimal and low-value, the customer enjoys the packing process (some do — it's a useful sorting/sentimental-review exercise for many people), or budget is genuinely tight and the £350–£700 premium is the difference between affording the move and not.

Fragile-only wins when: the customer wants to pack their own clothes-and-books (low-skill, low-risk) but wants professionals handling the kitchen-and-glass (where the damage cost on a single broken item can equal the fragile-only service fee), the move is mid-size (2-3 bed family home with normal household contents), the customer has 1–2 weeks before move day for the routine packing but not the bandwidth for the fragile work. This is the most-popular tier and right for most family moves.

Fragile-only loses when: the move is genuinely tiny (no real fragile content) or the move is very large (where the saving from DIY-ing the clothes and books is small relative to the total bill). For very large moves with valuable contents, full pack is usually better value despite the higher headline price.

DIY wins when: the move is small or short-distance, budget is the dominant concern, the customer has plenty of pre-move time to do the work, the contents are predominantly clothes and books (low fragile content), or the customer wants the sorting-and-decluttering benefit that comes naturally with self-packing. See our broader DIY-vs-pro comparison.

DIY loses when: the customer underestimates the time required (most people do — a typical 3-bed home takes 25–40 hours of focused packing work, often spread across 2–3 weekends), the household has more fragile content than the customer realises, or the insurance gap on self-packed items matters (covered below).

Section 4

Inside our process for a Staffordshire packing service

A booked packing service starts with the same in-person home survey we use for every removal quote. The lead-hand walks the property noting room contents, flagging anything particularly fragile or valuable that needs the dish-pack carton or the picture-frame pack rather than standard boxes, identifying any items the customer wants to handle personally (so we don't pack them by accident), and estimating the time and materials required.

The packing visit itself usually happens 1–2 days before move day, sometimes the morning of move day for smaller fragile-only jobs. The crew arrives in branded uniform with a full materials kit on the van: standard cartons in three sizes, book boxes, dish-pack cartons with cell dividers, wardrobe boxes, picture-frame packs, archival kraft paper, acid-free tissue for fine items, bubble wrap, low-tack tape, marker pens, room-destination stickers, and the inventory clipboard.

Work order within a typical 3-bed pack: kitchen first (most fragile, most time-consuming, usually 2–3 hours), then study and library (books are heavy but quick), then bedrooms (clothes go straight from wardrobe to wardrobe-box, drawer contents emptied carefully), then bathrooms (mostly fast — toiletries and cabinets), then loft and garage (variable depending on storage style), then the smaller specialist categories (art, mirrors, instruments, electronics) handled separately with the right protective materials.

What you should do during the pack visit: be home for the start (so the crew can ask any questions and you can point out anything that needs personal handling), stay reachable by phone (small questions come up mid-pack — usually 'where do you want this kept for personal handling?' kind of things), and ideally clear a corner of one room for the 'personal handling' pile that you'll pack yourself. You don't need to be present throughout — most customers leave the crew to work and come back for the final sign-off.

End of pack visit: the crew walks through the inventory with you, asks about anything ambiguous, hands over the signed inventory schedule, and confirms move-day arrangements. The packed cartons sit safely in the property overnight (or for the 1–2 day gap if packing is two days ahead), ready for the loading crew on the morning of move day. Section 5

Materials — what we use and why

The materials a professional packing crew brings are noticeably different from what you'd typically buy in a high-street packing-supplies kit, and the difference materially affects the quality of the pack.

Cartons in three standard sizes. Standard moving boxes (about 2 cu ft), book boxes (about 1.5 cu ft — smaller because books packed full into a standard box are too heavy to lift safely), and large boxes (about 4 cu ft for lighter bulky items like bedding and soft furnishings). Many DIY packers reach for standard boxes for everything and end up with split cartons or overpacked book boxes that risk back injury on lift day. We grade the carton to the contents at pack time.

Dish-pack cartons with internal cell dividers. Specialised cartons designed for plates, bowls and glassware with internal corrugated dividers that keep items individually compartmentalised. Each cell holds one wrapped item; the dividers prevent contact damage during loading and transit. Costs about 3x what a standard carton costs but the breakage-rate difference on a typical kitchen pack is enormous. Available from our packing shop if you want them for self-pack work.

Wardrobe boxes. Tall cartons with an internal hanger rail. Clothes go from wardrobe to wardrobe box on the hangers, eliminating the fold-then-iron cycle that DIY packing usually involves for hanging clothes. We collect the wardrobe boxes on move day; the customer doesn't keep them.

Archival kraft paper as the contact layer. Plain unprinted kraft paper for wrapping items where direct contact matters — china, silverware, polished surfaces. Newsprint (with its printing-ink residue) is the worst choice for any item with a polished or porous finish; we never use it. See more on fragile packing techniques.

Acid-free tissue for fine items. For wedding china, antique porcelain, fine silverware, and any item with delicate finish, we use acid-free archival tissue as the contact layer before kraft paper or bubble wrap. The acid-free tissue prevents long-term contact damage to vulnerable finishes — relevant for items that may go into storage for some weeks before being unpacked at destination.

Marker pens, room stickers, inventory clipboard. Every carton is marked with room of origin, contents summary, destination room, and inventory reference number. The labelling system is what makes the unpack at destination smooth — without it, the customer is opening every box to find the kettle.

Section 6

Insurance — the packed-items-coverage gap

This is the area where the full-pack vs self-pack decision has the biggest hidden consequence, and most customers don't think about it until something gets damaged in transit.

Items packed by our crew: covered under standard Goods in Transit insurance (£50,000 per consignment) for both external damage to the carton AND internal damage to the contents. If a wrapped wine glass arrives broken inside a sealed carton, the insurance pays out — we packed it, we own the packing standard, the claim is straightforward.

Items packed by the customer: covered for external damage only — if the carton itself is visibly damaged in transit, the contents claim is straightforward. If the carton arrives externally intact but the contents are broken inside, the claim is generally rejected because the insurer (reasonably) takes the position that whatever broke the contents must have happened during the customer's packing, not the professional handling. This is a standard exclusion across the UK removals insurance market, not specific to us.

The practical implication: if you self-pack items where internal-damage cover matters (china, glass, ceramics, art, electronics), you're effectively self-insuring those items. For low-value contents that's fine. For high-value contents — wedding china, family silver, art collection, expensive audio equipment — the cost of the fragile-only packing service often pays for itself if a single significant item turns up broken.

The international moving wrinkle: on international moves the insurance position is even tighter — marine insurance frequently excludes ALL self-packed cartons from cover, internal AND external damage. For international moves we strongly recommend full packing service rather than fragile-only. See more on international moves specifically.

Declared-value uplifts for high-value packed items: for any item over £1,500 individual value, we recommend a declared-value top-up on top of standard cover (premium typically 1–2% of declared value for the move). The declared-value process is the same regardless of whether we pack or you pack — but for self-packed declared items, the internal-damage exclusion still applies. Section 8

Combining packing services with other parts of your move

Most professional packing isn't booked standalone — it's part of a wider move package. Common combinations and the small-print of how they fit together.

Packing + residential removal. The most common combination — full or fragile-only pack the day before, then the standard residential removal on move day. The pack visit and the move day are usually quoted together as a single fixed price.

Packing + storage. Packing the day before, loading and transporting to our palletised storage depot, holding for the storage period, then delivering and unpacking at the new property when the chain completes. Common pattern for customers with completion-date uncertainty.

Packing + international move. Full packing is essentially mandatory for international shipments because of the insurance position on self-packed cartons. The pack happens 1–3 days before the container loads at our depot. International removals page has the wider context.

Packing + antiques service. For households with significant antique content, the packing crew works alongside our antiques-specialist crew — standard household items get the regular pack treatment; antiques get the specialist wrapping, crating and inventory protocol. Both are coordinated by the same project manager.

Packing + piano move. The piano itself doesn't need packing in the conventional sense (it's wrapped and dollyed by the piano crew on move day) but the rest of the household often goes alongside on the same job. We coordinate the packing visit, the piano move, and the household removal as a single logistical package.

Common questions

How much does professional packing cost in Staffordshire in 2026?

Full pack add-ons to a standard residential removal: studio/1-bed £180–£300; 2-bed £250–£420; 3-bed £350–£700; 4-bed £550–£1,100. Fragile-only pack: 2-bed £120–£220, 3-bed £180–£380, 4-bed £280–£550. Single-room packs (kitchen-only, master-bedroom-only) £80–£180 per room. Materials supply only (no labour) £40–£280 depending on house size.

Full pack or fragile-only pack — which should I choose?

Most family moves are best served by fragile-only pack — our crew handles the kitchen, glass, art and ceramics (highest skill, highest damage cost); you handle the clothes and books (low-skill, low-risk DIY). Full pack wins for time-pressured households, 4-bed+ properties, international moves (insurance reasons), and households with substantial fragile content. DIY wins for studio/1-bed moves with low fragile content.

Does the packing service include unpacking at the new property?

Not as standard — unpacking is a separate add-on, typically 60–80% of the original pack cost. Most customers don't book unpacking because they prefer to unpack at their own pace once they've decided where things go in the new home. The exceptions are white-glove moves, time-pressured professionals and customers with limited physical capacity. White-glove includes full unpack as standard.

What's the insurance position on items I pack myself?

Self-packed cartons are covered for EXTERNAL damage only — if the carton itself is visibly damaged in transit, the claim is straightforward. Internal damage (e.g. wrapped wine glass arrives broken inside an intact carton) is generally excluded because the insurer takes the position that the breakage happened during your packing, not professional handling. Items packed by our crew are covered for both external AND internal damage. International marine cover often excludes ALL self-packed cartons entirely.

How far ahead should I book a packing visit?

4–6 weeks ahead is comfortable for any May–September move; 2–3 weeks ahead is usually fine for the off-peak months. The pack visit itself happens 1–2 days before move day. For full-house packs (4+ bedrooms) we sometimes spread the work across two days; for smaller fragile-only packs, the visit is typically half a day. Survey-to-quote turnaround is 5–7 working days.
