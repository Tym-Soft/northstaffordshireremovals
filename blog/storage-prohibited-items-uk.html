What you can't store in self storage — the UK 2026 rules

Every storage provider in the UK — self-storage operators, full-service warehouses, even your own garage if you're using it as long-term storage — has a list of items they won't accept. Some restrictions are legal (you genuinely can't store certain things by UK law); some are insurance-driven (your storage provider's cover specifically excludes them); some are practical (the items damage other stored contents). We've turned away enough items at our Stoke-on-Trent depot over the years to recognise the patterns, and we've fielded enough customer questions about prohibitions to know which restrictions catch first-time storage users out. This guide walks through what you can't store, why, what happens if you try, and what to do with prohibited items instead.

Section 1

The seven categories of items prohibited by every UK storage provider

1. Flammable and combustible items. Petrol, diesel, paraffin, lighter fluid, propane and butane cylinders (full or even allegedly empty), fireworks of any size, lit candles, paint and paint thinners in significant quantity (small household-decorating amounts are sometimes permitted; check), aerosol cans in bulk, road flares, charcoal in significant volume. Fire risk in a closed warehouse is catastrophic — a single ignition can affect dozens of customers' belongings. Universal prohibition across the storage industry.

2. Hazardous and toxic materials. Asbestos in any form (including older insulation materials and certain Artex ceiling treatments), corrosive substances (battery acid, bleach in bulk, drain cleaner concentrate), pesticides and herbicides in commercial quantities, chemical lab supplies, mercury thermometers (modern alcohol-based replacements are fine), tyres being stored long-term (a fire risk plus they degrade other stored items). Universal prohibition.

3. Perishable food and live items. All perishable food, anything in a tin or jar past its use-by date, live plants (most can't survive long-term storage and several attract pests), live animals of any kind (universal prohibition — never an option), insects or insect-related items being preserved. Universal prohibition because of pest-attraction risk.

4. Weapons and ammunition. Firearms (any working firearm requires specific licensed storage outside the self-storage system), ammunition (universal prohibition regardless of legality of the underlying firearm), large bladed weapons (some swords and antique blades are sometimes permitted in declared-inventory storage; check), explosives of any kind including legal display fireworks. Universal prohibition.

5. Illegal items. Controlled drugs, stolen property, counterfeit goods being sold, fake currency, illegal copies of copyrighted material in commercial quantities. Self-storage operators cooperate fully with law enforcement and have the legal right to inspect units with reasonable suspicion of illegal contents.

6. Human and animal remains. Human remains (universal prohibition; specific funeral-industry storage is the only legal route), pet remains being preserved (cremated remains are usually permitted; un-cremated remains generally aren't), medical specimens, biological waste. Universal prohibition.

7. Cash, securities, jewellery beyond declared limits. Most storage providers limit cash storage to a token amount (typically £100); securities (share certificates, bonds), unmounted gemstones, high-value jewellery beyond a declared limit (usually £1,000-£5,000) require specific arrangements — usually a bank safe-deposit box rather than general storage. Insurance position: cash, securities and high-value jewellery are typically EXCLUDED from standard storage insurance regardless of whether the storage provider technically accepts them.

Section 2

Items that are PRACTICALLY restricted but technically allowed

Beyond the universally-prohibited categories, a second tier of items is technically allowed by most providers but practically problematic. Many storage providers will accept them but with explicit warnings about what could go wrong.

Fabric and leather items in long-term storage can develop mould or surface damage if stored in unclimated facilities through winter months. Climate-stable storage (our facility runs 10°C-22°C year-round) handles this fine; unheated self-storage facilities can damage stored fabric over multi-month winters. Our storage security guide covers the climate-control question.

Old electronics (vintage TVs with CRT screens, old computer monitors, leaded batteries in old equipment) often contain trace hazardous materials that come under WEEE recycling regulations. Most storage providers accept them but you'll need to dispose of them through proper WEEE channels when storage ends — they can't go to general waste.

Open containers of household liquids (half-used bottles of bleach, an opened tin of paint, a part-full bottle of olive oil) are usually permitted but discouraged. Spillage during transport or storage can damage other items in the same unit. Either dispose of, finish using, or seal carefully and accept the small spill risk.

Items with strong odours (musty old clothes, items from a smoking household, items from a household with cats) transfer their odour to other stored items in the same warehouse over weeks and months. We'll accept these items but warn customers about the odour-transfer issue.

Items with potential pest infestation (clothes, soft furnishings, books from a household with known moth or silverfish problems) need treatment before storage to avoid contaminating other stored items. We sometimes refuse items that arrive with visible infestation evidence and recommend treatment before re-attempting storage.

Section 3

Why these restrictions exist — beyond legal compliance

Storage prohibition lists look bureaucratic and over-cautious from the outside. The history behind each restriction is usually a specific past incident that left the storage industry, the insurance industry, or both, paying out claims they couldn't recover from. Knowing the why makes the rules less arbitrary.

Flammables prohibition comes directly from warehouse-fire incidents. A single propane cylinder leaking in a corner unit ignited by an electrical fault has destroyed entire storage facilities multiple times in the UK over the last 20 years. Insurance industry response: blanket flammable-item exclusion in storage cover. Storage industry response: blanket prohibition.

Perishable-item prohibition comes from pest contamination incidents. A single forgotten box of food in a unit attracts rodents, the rodents spread to neighbouring units, the eventual pest-treatment cost runs into thousands of pounds, and damaged customer items create insurance claims. Universal prohibition is cheaper than incident-by-incident recovery.

Weapons and ammunition prohibition is partly legal (specific licensing required for firearms storage) and partly insurance-driven (standard storage cover excludes weapons-related claims). Even unloaded firearms in storage create incident-response complications that the industry has decided collectively not to underwrite.

Cash and high-value items prohibition is purely insurance-driven. The insurance industry has decided storage-rate claims for cash and unmounted jewellery are too easy to fabricate, so all standard storage policies exclude these items. Storage providers prohibit accepting them rather than having customers store uninsured items they think are insured.

The practical takeaway: the rules exist because the industry learned from past mistakes. Compliance is in your interest as well as ours.

Section 4

What to do with items that CAN'T be stored

Flammable household chemicals: Staffordshire County Council operates household waste recycling centres at Hanley, Lichfield, Stafford and several smaller locations that accept household-quantities of paint, paint thinner, lighter fluid, and similar materials free of charge from local residents. Commercial quantities require licensed-waste-carrier collection.

Propane and butane cylinders: most Calor Gas dealers will take back used cylinders for refilling or proper disposal. Don't attempt to dispose of cylinders through general waste — they're high-risk in waste-vehicle fires.

Old electronics under WEEE: Staffordshire County Council household waste sites accept all small WEEE for free; larger items (fridges, freezers, washing machines) can be collected through council bulky-waste collection at modest cost. Several Staffordshire charities also accept working electronics for refurbishment programmes.

Pesticides and garden chemicals: household-quantity garden chemicals go through the council household waste sites; larger commercial quantities through licensed waste carriers.

Firearms requiring secure storage during a move or while your premises are being refurbished: speak to your local Staffordshire Police firearms department; they can advise on temporary licensed-storage options.

High-value jewellery and cash during a move: bank safe-deposit boxes are the right answer for high-value items needing secure short-term storage. Most major UK banks offer them at branch level; some specialist providers (Vaultex, Metropolitan Safe Deposits) offer larger capacity for serious collections.

Live plants you can't move with you: several Staffordshire garden centres and charity gardens accept healthy plant donations. Don't risk live plants in storage — they'll die, attract pests, and create cleanup costs for your eventual storage exit.

Perishable food remaining at move-out: donate unopened items to a local food bank (Staffordshire has several active food bank networks — Trussell Trust operates the main ones in Stoke, Hanley and Stafford); compost or dispose of opened items. Section 5

What happens if you try to store a prohibited item

Storage providers handle prohibition differently depending on what's discovered and when.

Discovered at pack/collection. Best-case outcome: we politely refuse the item, you arrange disposal or alternative storage. Worst-case for the customer: a delay to the pack day while we wait for the item to be moved off-site. This is by far the most common discovery point and we handle it as a routine part of the pack visit.

Discovered at warehouse entry. If a prohibited item is somehow not caught at the pack visit but is identified during loading at our warehouse, the item is set aside in our refusal-area and the customer notified immediately. The customer either collects the item within 7 days or it's disposed of via the appropriate channel at the customer's cost. Storage of the wider consignment continues normally.

Discovered during storage (e.g. a perishable item starts to smell, or an item is found to have caused damage to surrounding items): immediate customer contact, item disposed of properly, customer may be liable for any damage caused to other customers' items. This is the worst-outcome scenario and is also the rarest because of our pack-visit and warehouse-entry checks.

Discovered during a customer-facing event (e.g. police inspection, fire investigation, insurance audit): the storage provider must cooperate with the investigating authority. For illegal items, this typically means law-enforcement involvement and possible criminal proceedings against the customer. For dangerous items not yet causing harm, this means insurance-driven cleanup and customer financial liability.

The contractual position: our storage terms and conditions (signed at booking) explicitly authorise inspection of units where we have reasonable cause to believe prohibited items are stored. We don't routinely inspect unit contents — that would breach customer privacy expectations — but we reserve the right to do so for safety reasons.

Section 6

Storage-provider-specific restrictions worth knowing

Beyond the universal prohibitions, individual storage providers have their own specific restrictions. Always ask for the full storage-terms document before booking; the headlines below are common but not universal.

Vehicle storage varies wildly. Some self-storage facilities accept car and motorcycle storage in specific drive-in units; most don't. Liquids in stored vehicles (fuel tank, brake fluid, oil) are usually required to be drained or minimised. Specialist classic-car storage providers exist separately from general self-storage and are the right route for valuable or sensitive vehicles.

Tyre storage is increasingly restricted because of fire risk and environmental regulations on tyre stockpiling. Most professional storage providers limit stored tyres to a maximum count per unit (typically 4-8) and require them to be inflated and not stored stacked horizontally for extended periods.

Refrigerators and freezers in storage must be fully defrosted, dried, and propped open at the door before storage begins. Doors closed on a damp interior cause mould rapidly. Some storage providers refuse to accept white goods that haven't been properly prepared.

Mattresses can be stored but should be in a sealed mattress storage cover to prevent dust and pest contamination. Stored mattresses long-term (12+ months) should be turned periodically — most storage providers won't do this for you but will allow appointment access for the customer to do it.

Documents and books are universally permitted but climate-stable storage is strongly recommended for long-term retention. Paper degrades fast in damp or temperature-variable environments. Our downsizing storage guide covers paper-heavy storage in more detail.

Plants, animals, anything alive: universal prohibition reiterated here because customers occasionally try anyway. We will refuse and the policy isn't negotiable.

Section 7

Pre-storage checklist — items to actually remove from your inventory

Before any pack visit, run through your inventory and remove or set aside the following categories. Saves time on pack day and avoids embarrassing discoveries during loading.

Kitchen and pantry: all perishable food (donate unopened to food banks, compost opened); part-bottles of oils, vinegars, condiments; opened bottles of cleaning products; aerosol cooking sprays; full or part-full propane camping cylinders.

Garage and workshop: all petrol cans (drain and dispose properly), paint cans (recycling centre), thinners and solvents, garden chemicals and pesticides, full or empty propane cylinders, charcoal in volume, fireworks if any, automotive batteries, all sharp blade tools needing specific safe-transport arrangements.

Garden and outbuildings: live plants (donate or compost), live garden chemicals, garden waste, opened weedkillers, fuel for petrol mowers and trimmers (drain), gas cylinders for BBQs.

Bathroom and medicine cabinet: expired medications (return to pharmacy for proper disposal), expired cosmetics, opened bottles of bleach and toilet cleaner, aerosol products in bulk.

General throughout the home: matches and lighters in significant quantity, all kinds of candles meant to be lit, batteries past their expiry, anything containing mercury (some old thermometers, certain medical equipment), suspected damaged or leaking electronics with leaking batteries.

Safes and valuables: consider whether high-value items (jewellery, watches, cash, securities, important documents) should travel with you personally rather than going into storage. Most are EXCLUDED from standard storage insurance regardless of provider acceptance.

FAQ

Common questions

What can't I put in self storage?

Seven universal prohibitions across UK storage: flammable items (petrol, propane, fireworks), hazardous materials (asbestos, corrosives, bulk pesticides), perishable food, weapons and ammunition, illegal items, human/animal remains, and cash or unmounted high-value jewellery above declared limits. Plus storage-provider-specific restrictions (varying by provider).

Why can't I store paint or aerosols?

Fire risk in a closed warehouse is catastrophic — a single ignition can affect dozens of customers' units. Small household quantities of dry paint cans are sometimes permitted but commercial quantities or paint thinners always prohibited. Aerosols in bulk are universally prohibited because of pressurised-container fire risk.

Can I store my fridge or freezer?

Yes, but they must be fully defrosted, dried, and propped open at the door before storage begins. Doors closed on a damp interior cause mould rapidly. Most storage providers refuse to accept white goods that haven't been properly prepared.

What should I do with prohibited items I need to get rid of before storage?

Staffordshire County Council recycling centres at Hanley, Lichfield and Stafford accept household-quantities of paint, garden chemicals, aerosols and small WEEE free of charge. Propane cylinders go back to Calor Gas dealers. Charity food banks accept unopened perishable food. Our decluttering guide covers wider pre-move clearing.

What happens if I accidentally try to store a prohibited item?

Most prohibited items are caught at the pack visit and politely refused — you arrange disposal or alternative storage. If discovered at warehouse entry, the item is set aside in a refusal area and you collect within 7 days. Storage of the wider consignment continues normally. We don't routinely inspect units but reserve the right to do so for safety reasons.
